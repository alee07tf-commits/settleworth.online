Real data

What drives a nerve damage settlement

There is no reliable published "average" settlement for nerve damage — figures vary too much by nerve, severity and permanence. What the medical literature does show is why these claims value high: traumatic peripheral nerve injuries frequently cause lasting disability, which pushes the pain-and-suffering multiplier toward the top of its range. Permanent nerve damage versus temporary

This is the only distinction that materially changes the number, and it is a medical question with a documentary answer rather than a matter of how the injury feels. A neurapraxia — a bruised or compressed nerve with the sheath intact — usually recovers over weeks to months, and it is valued as a temporary injury however unpleasant it was. An axonotmesis or neurotmesis, where the fibres themselves or the whole nerve are torn, regenerates slowly at best and often incompletely. That is what a claim in the top of the multiplier range is built on.

The document that settles the argument is an EMG or nerve-conduction study, usually repeated. A single study soon after the injury shows the damage; a second one months later shows whether it is resolving, and it is the comparison between them that an insurer accepts or disputes. If you have both, set the severity in the calculator from what the later study says. If you only have one, and it is recent, the honest position is that the permanence is not yet established — which is also a reason not to close a claim early, because the value of a nerve injury cannot be fixed before the medical picture is.

Adjust for your state

State fault rules and nerve injury claims

Nerve-injury claims still turn on fault, and your state's rule applies. What pushes the value up is permanence — proven with EMG and nerve-conduction studies — but the state can still reduce it for shared fault.

Pure comparative

Recover even if mostly at fault; your award is cut by your %. e.g. California, Washington, New York.

Modified — 50% bar

No recovery if you are 50% or more at fault. e.g. Colorado, Georgia, Tennessee.

Modified — 51% bar

No recovery if you are 51% or more at fault. e.g. Texas, Illinois, Pennsylvania.

Pure contributory

Any fault at all can bar recovery. Only AL, MD, NC, VA & DC.

Where the ten largest states sit: California, New York and Washington apply pure comparative fault, so a share of blame cuts the total but never bars it. Texas, Illinois, Pennsylvania, Ohio and Florida — Florida since its 2023 reform — bar recovery at 51% or more. Georgia bars it at 50%. North Carolina and Virginia are contributory-negligence states where any share of fault at all can end the claim. Set your state in the calculator and it applies the right one for you.
